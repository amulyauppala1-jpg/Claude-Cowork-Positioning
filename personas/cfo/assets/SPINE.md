# Campaign spine — CFO

Fixed before writing anything. All three assets hold to it; that consistency is
the point of building them as a set rather than one at a time.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Persona** | CFO — `personas/cfo/README.md` |
| **Motion** | Enterprise rollout → `roles_by_motion: economic_buyer`. They hold the budget and decide. |
| **Lead argument** | **`vp-coverage`** — one deployment, one vendor, every function |
| **Second** | `vp-configured` — answers the adoption fear |
| **Omitted** | `vp-executes` — true, but the claim every AI vendor has made to this person. Leading with capability spends credibility instead of building it. |
| **Proof** | >90% of Cowork usage isn't software development (Anthropic blog, 2026-07-07). 60 days old, inside the 90-day ceiling. |
| **Core phrase** | *"One deployment, not another line item."* |
| **CTA** | Get Cowork for your team |

**Why `vp-coverage` leads.** Consolidation is the CFO's own word for a problem
they already have. It reframes Cowork from a new line item into a way to collapse
several — the single most useful move available at this desk, and the opposite of
what the product file's default order would produce.

**Why that proof point.** `vp-coverage` claims one deployment covers everyone.
Usage being >90% non-development is the evidence that it does. The other proof
points are stronger in isolation but don't support *this* claim.

**Vocabulary steered around** (from the persona's Avoid list): productivity,
efficiency without a number, transformation, and any headcount or FTE framing —
the last because a CFO will hold you to it at renewal.

- **No pricing content exists.** For a CFO this is blocking, not cosmetic: the
